THE COMPLETE CHECKLIST

Every factor considered

Some items set pressure, some measure performance, and some check reliability. They do not all add points. Unmeasured and unused ideas are listed separately.

Data and scorekeeping 11 items
  • Unchanged source files; correct match, team and player identities
  • Delivery order and a separate legal-ball count
  • Numeric extras; team runs versus bowler runs
  • Credited wickets; no guessed run-out or retirement blame
  • Scoreboard, innings-ending, ball-count and result-margin checks
  • Rain and revised-target timing
  • Missing information stays unknown, not zero
  • Usable performances in no-result matches
  • Earlier dates only; no same-day or future information
  • Recorded facts, chosen weights and estimates kept separate
  • Full scorecards retained beside competitive-play totals
Before the match 21 items
  • Competition type and importance: IPL, senior, qualifying and emerging cricket
  • Gradual competition-importance adjustment
  • Championship finals
  • Elimination, Eliminators, semi-finals and a place in the final
  • Qualifier 1 and a genuine second chance
  • Series deciders, survival, early wins, undecided series and dead rubbers
  • Earlier standings, remaining fixtures and win/loss consequences
  • Qualifying versus finishing in the top two
  • The practical IPL 14-point target, not guaranteed qualification
  • Different stakes for the two teams
  • Defending-champion expectations
  • Favourite expectations from earlier team results
  • Longer-term team history, excluding the latest five results
  • Home expectations only when verified; inactive in this dataset
  • Strongest expectation only; expectation/form/rivalry share an 8-point limit
  • Last five results compared with what was expected
  • Stated rivalry ratings and an explicit default
  • Opponent strength, tempered when history is limited
  • Past meetings versus earlier expectations, not raw head-to-head wins
  • Fixture uncertainty, replacement matches and announcement dates
  • Possible versus guaranteed qualification; uncertain net-run-rate ties
Player responsibility 8 items
  • Captain responsibility
  • Verified vice-captain responsibility; never both leadership roles
  • Earlier batting and bowling ability, calculated separately
  • Likely balls faced/bowled and expected share of the team's job
  • Remaining batting strength after dismissed players are removed
  • Bowler quotas, the current over and feasible non-consecutive future overs
  • Confirmed XI, observed alternatives and flagged missing-data fallbacks
  • Reliance capped at 25%, combined responsibility at 35%, player pressure at 100
Before each delivery 18 items
  • Score, target and runs needed
  • Legal balls left; powerplay, middle overs and death overs
  • Wickets in hand and shortage of batting options
  • Projected first-innings total versus the venue's usual total
  • Required rate versus normal scoring pace
  • The bowler's own run allowance while defending
  • New versus settled striker
  • Wickets lost close together
  • Consecutive legal team dot balls
  • Recent boundaries and four-plus-run balls
  • Partnership runs, balls and resets after wickets
  • Free hits and their effect on runs, wickets and pressure
  • A brief hitting burst versus sustaining a high rate
  • Reduced weight for very easy or near-impossible chases
  • 35% pre-match pressure plus 65% in-match pressure
  • Starting-task pressure; no extra reward for self-created difficulty
  • When sixes alone can no longer win or tie the chase
  • Extras or unusual runs reopening a chase; clinching balls and errors still count
Runs and wickets 14 items
  • Comparable role, competition, season, innings, phase and batting position
  • Earlier competition and venue scoring rates
  • The current opponent's earlier batting or bowling ability
  • Useful chase runs capped at the remaining need
  • Wicket value from balls left, wickets left and batter ability
  • The player's earlier ordinary-pressure performance
  • Runs scored/saved and wickets, charged to the correct player
  • Improvement over usual performance, limited by available history
  • A late highlight cannot erase a poor whole innings or spell
  • Repeated good, neutral and poor contributions, not team win percentage
  • Continuous pressure weights above 50; high-pressure label from 60
  • Limited weight per match; small samples stay closer to neutral
  • 50% situation-adjusted quality, 30% above-usual play, 20% consistency
  • First-ball failures remain in the record
Skill and errors 10 items
  • Valid delivery labels; pace/spin consistency; doubtful labels left ungraded
  • Wides, no-balls and confirmed beamers take priority over good-looking outcomes
  • Effective yorkers, variations and ordinary deliveries; death-over context
  • Useful batting responses, difficult balls and defence during a rebuild
  • Scoring after dots and recovery after boundaries; strongest bonus only
  • Full-toss uncertainty: modest effective-ball credit, boundary costs, no guessed height
  • No automatic easy-ball batting bonus for full tosses; illegal-opportunity safeguards
  • Earlier similar deliveries and personal history, matched to the task
  • Big-match, death-over, severe, collapse, dot-chain and run-leak situations
  • Annotation coverage, limited match weight and small-sample adjustment
Big-match performances 14 items
  • Own-team pre-match pressure above 70; importance weights both good and poor play
  • The full competitive innings or spell, not just dramatic balls
  • Run target fixed at the player's first opportunity in each over
  • Normal scoring pace when batting first
  • Required chase pace with limited blame for an earlier shortfall
  • The tighter of normal economy and the runs affordable while defending
  • Limited secondary wicket value and bounded batting-survival credit
  • A real, still-live collapse with time to rebuild
  • Enough personal runs/balls and partnership growth or run share
  • Positive rebuilding quality; half-credit protection capped at 10
  • Inherited defensive difficulty, enough balls and genuinely controlled scoring
  • Individual failure or verified help in a win, including closing actions
  • Small result adjustment: at most 2 big-match points or 0.6 final points
  • Match-importance weighting and a three-match starting evidence allowance
Eligibility and reliability 14 items
  • At least six relevant matches, 60 legal balls and 30 ordinary-play events
  • At least four effective appearances; evidence spread across matches
  • Positive full-performance quality plus supported pressure-ball or big-match results
  • Separate score and percentile; the 50/20/30 mix is not a win probability
  • Separate batting/bowling lists, unique IDs and no player-specific overrides
  • 95% individual score ranges from 250 match redraws
  • Joint rank and top-ten stability from 1,000 shared redraws; tied places handled fairly
  • Sensitivity to weights, years, competition scope, innings and recent form
  • Earlier-to-later comparisons; eligibility based only on earlier matches
  • Independent reconstruction of scores, components and source totals
  • Seeded test cases, unit/functional/regression tests and cricket-law checks
  • Remove-one-match checks and both positive and negative match examples
  • Rejected alternatives retained as evidence, not silently promoted
  • Dataset coverage, submission names and portal eligibility checked separately
Considered, but not separately scored 25 items
  • Actual anxiety, confidence or a claimed psychological cause
  • Measured crowd hostility, media attention or publicity
  • Rarity, prize money, national emotion or upset stories
  • Avoiding a whitewash or facing a former team
  • Exact net-run-rate, winning-margin or chase-by-over targets
  • Live updates from other matches and bonus-point targets
  • Exact field positions, boundary sizes, timeouts and over-rate penalties
  • Actual pitch, weather, dew, wet ball and venue familiarity
  • Unverified DLS timing, reduced budgets and restart stress
  • Super Overs mixed with regular innings
  • Guessed effects of dropped catches, misfields, DRS or disputed calls
  • Unmeasured injury, fatigue, travel or recovery condition
  • Debuts, first finals, selection pressure, farewells, milestones or awards
  • Private plans, strike farming, assigned roles or intended tail protection
  • Unobserved future bowling plans or forced matchups
  • Separate handedness, unfamiliar-role, promotion or personal-familiarity penalties
  • Extra boundary-drought, rising-required-rate or expensive-over bonuses
  • Earlier flat pre-match weighting
  • Replacing the current score with a win-probability model
  • Unpromoted rebuild rules or direct match-importance weights
  • Recent-form-only or time-decayed rankings as the final selection
  • Resampling frequency or mean-minus-standard-deviation as the rank key
  • Contracts, auction prices, nationality and overseas-player limits
  • External performance-data merges
  • Using this ball's result, or a fixed top quarter, to label its prior pressure

HOW TO READ THE RESULT

Three parts. One transparent score.

50%

Runs and wickets

How good was the contribution for that situation? Was it above the player's usual level? Did it happen repeatedly?

20%

Skill and errors

Effective deliveries and batting responses, recovery after setbacks, and discipline. A label alone is not proof of good or poor technique.

30%

Big matches

The full competitive innings or spell when the team's pre-match stakes are high, including earned rebuilding and defensive credit.

What the score does not claim

This is a historical, situation-based index, not measured anxiety or a calibrated chance of winning. Small samples and different competition mixes matter. The archive is not complete career history and contains no T20 World Cup data. The checks are not a blind external test or proof of predictive superiority.

For the detailed algorithm, refer to the report.
